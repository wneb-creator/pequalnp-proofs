The examples show that zero syntactic mixed-OR count need not imply semantic
monochromaticity; arbitrary AND bypass may increase that count; a serving star
can be unused; a top AND operand need not be a pure disjunction; one centre can
have disconnected witness components; and an edge-active circuit can have five
semantically mixed OR gates but only three AND gates. These examples do not
contradict the posted lower bound.

MixedSupport also proves that a monotone signal accepting singleton inputs of
both bipartition colours has no singleton-accepting safe context; every surviving
pair is accepted by that context with both singleton inputs rejected. This does
not justify bypassing the signal: it may still filter nonedges.

ProductiveOr proves an abstract binary-wire base case: if both children of each
OR accept some singleton and each OR has monochromatic singleton support, then
every nonempty-support wire depends on one colour and every cross-pair relation
is rectangular. A safe rectangular product in a C4-free graph accepts a star.
The wire recurrence and readback assumptions are explicit; this is not a proof
that arbitrary circuits satisfy the extra OR conditions.

The C6 CNF example satisfies AO2, exact output, T0, serving-star witnesses, and
edge activity. Every AND operand is true on every co-singleton assignment, so
fixing any one input false cannot erase an AND by constant-false propagation.
Only the final gate is safe; every earlier gate accepts a forbidden diagonal.
Independent evaluation also checks that every gate lies in the output cone.

Hall gives an exact counterexample to a fixed-orientation matching assignment:
two matching edges have the same sole eligible AND gate when eligibility uses
only point-row cofactors and raw line reach. This does not refute the repaired
doubled-slot argument, which also uses the opposite orientation, or the gap.

CrossRectangleCover and BooleanFactorCover prove seven conditional cover lemmas.
A C4-free union of a baseline rectangle and two nested cross rectangles has a
cover of size at most two. The Boolean wrappers give the same bound for three
explicit factorization equations, covering the empty-support, one-colour, and
mixed-support exceptional-OR cases. Independent exact-diff mathematical and
source review passed; all seven lemmas use only the three standard axioms.

CutConstruction.indexed_one_exceptional_or_cover constructs its own cut evaluator

FullProductNesting proves that if one graph-existence function is exactly the
conjunction of two graph-existence functions, its edge relation is their
intersection and the two neighborhoods are nested at every row and column.
The proof uses assignments with two vertices on one side and one on the other.
It does not assume arbitrary circuit factors are graph-existence functions.
The row and column controller lemmas additionally handle arbitrary families of
monotone factors: exact conjunction equality selects one factor that agrees
with the whole neighborhood cofactor on every assignment of the other side.
The selected factor may depend on the fixed vertex.

OwnerStrictness and IndependentFactors prove a strict matching bound for two
monotone factors with exact graph-function conjunction and rectangular pair
terms: a nonempty diagonal matching has size at most the total tagged term
count minus one. Individual factors need not be graph-existence functions.
The C4 condition and full assignment equality are explicit. This does not
count the cost of sharing or resolve arbitrary dependent exceptional OR gates.

The owner core also proves that a doubly owned rectangle has a central edge
accepted by no other doubly owned rectangle, and excludes four distinct
factors that each have a doubly owned rectangle and at most one exceptional
rectangle with a row or column owner. This is the combinatorial core of the
many-factor deficit argument; a full arbitrary-circuit bound is not claimed.

The complete many-factor owner bound is also checked: for a nonempty matching
index type, its size plus the number of factor tags is at most the number of
rectangle terms plus one. All tag sizes are handled; singleton factors are
removed by redirecting universal owned rows and columns and recursing on the
number of tags. IndependentFactors.many_factor_matching_bound applies this to
arbitrary monotone factors with exact graph-function conjunction and rectangular
pair terms. No full graph-function assumption is imposed on individual factors.
This still requires the factor/rectangle representation; converting arbitrary
shared circuit signals to that representation at the required cost is open.
