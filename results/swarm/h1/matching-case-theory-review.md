Independent conditional review of the matching case

Status: a mathematical argument, not a Lean formalization and not a proof of the full gap. No circuit normalization or modification is asserted here.

Assume a strict, exactly fan-in-two AND/OR circuit computing the Petersen edge function, satisfying the actual `TopForm` definition, with four tops, no centreless top, exactly two dirty ANDs J1<J2, and |orN\lam|=2. Assume also that Ustar is the matching {23,05,69}; thus the tops' cross-pairs comprise twelve edges. These are conditional inputs to this review, not conclusions established here from an arbitrary circuit of size nineteen.

The definitions used are `lean/PEqualNP/Defs/TopForm.lean:99` (no positive AND strictly in any AND cone), `:133` (raw support through OR paths, stopping at ANDs), and `:152` (Lambda requires BOTH child supports nonempty and membership in a leaf OR cone). `IsAO2` at line 82 supplies valid earlier wires and exactly two operands; `orN` at line 123 means semantic failure to be below the graph function.

In this cubic graph with no four-cycle, each top has a one-vertex side and at most three vertices on its other side. Twelve distinct cross-edges from four tops force four full, disjoint three-stars. Their centres are K={1,4,7,8}: a centre outside K would cover its incident matching edge, contrary to Ustar. Their leaf supports are exactly N(k). Write R={0,2,3,5,6,9}. K is independent, each N(k) lies in R and meets every matching edge, and setting K=0 restricts the target to the three-edge matching on R.

Call a junk AND *visible* when it is reached from a centre operand through OR gates, stopping at the junk AND. Ordinary ancestry through the other junk AND does not count as visibility. After K=0, each centre operand is an OR of its visible junk sources. A true output on a matching edge traces backwards through OR gates to a positive AND top: a raw input cannot be an OR source of the output, since the target rejects every singleton. Consequently the three matching edges must be covered by the edge signatures of the visible junk sources.

For any visible J, choose a witnessing top with centre k. The function J AND OR(N(k)) is safe: its two factors are bounded by that top's centre and leaf operands. On each matching edge, OR(N(k)) is true, so this safe function retains exactly J's matching-edge signature.

The semantic counting fact is: a safe CNF of m linear OR-clauses accepting k disjoint matching edges has m≥2^k. Consider the 2^k assignments selecting one endpoint of each accepted edge and setting all other variables false. Each is negative and must falsify a clause. Every clause meets every accepted edge, so it can be false on at most one such selector. This argument also accommodates empty clauses; they cannot occur if any edge is accepted.

If only J1 is visible, J1 has two linear clauses: its AND cone contains no positive AND by TopForm and no earlier junk AND. The safe leaf filter adds one clause, so 2^k≤3 and J1 accepts at most one matching edge. If only J2 is visible, each operand of J2 is a linear OR, optionally OR'ed with J1. Writing J1=A AND B, distributivity expresses each operand using at most two linear clauses. J2 therefore has at most four clauses; its safe leaf filter gives at most five, so it accepts at most two matching edges.

The case where both are visible needs the following budget argument. Let Rc be ALL OR gates in the union of centre-side OR closures. Then Rc⊆orN\lam. Such a gate has raw support empty or {k} for a centre k. A nonempty support cannot occur in any leaf closure, whose support lies in R. An empty support fails Lambda's two-nonempty-children condition. The gate is dirty: its OR closure terminates at a raw centre or at a junk AND; positive AND terminals are excluded by TopForm. A dirty terminal's negative witness propagates through the OR path.

Both visible junk sources require at least two distinct gates in Rc. With only one OR gate, a nonempty centre support and both distinct junk terminals cannot all be connected by binary OR. Sharing that gate between centres does not create another input slot. Since |orN\lam|=2, this proves Rc=orN\lam and exhausts the entire budget.

Every Lambda gate is consequently a pure linear OR on R. Otherwise follow an OR path from a junk terminal towards that Lambda gate and select its first gate with nonempty raw support. Its junk-side child has empty support, so this gate is not Lambda; it is dirty and hence belongs to Rc. But its nonempty support lies both in K (by Rc membership) and in R (by its leaf ancestry), impossible. TopForm excludes positive AND terminals, so no nonlinear terminal remains.

Every OR inside an AND cone is dirty: its OR terminals are raw inputs or junk ANDs, since TopForm forbids positive AND terminals. Hence it belongs to Lambda or Rc. Inside J2's cone, an Rc gate cannot depend on J2 itself. After K=0 it therefore becomes either 0 or J1. It cannot be OR-mixed with a nonempty linear R-part: the mixing OR would neither be pure Lambda nor have Rc's support contained in K. Thus J2 is either a conjunction of two linear clauses, J1 AND one linear clause, J1 itself, or 0. In the independent case, the separate safe filters bound each visible junk's signature by one edge, giving at most two in their union. In every chained case J2's signature is contained in J1's, whose bound is one. None covers all three matching edges.

Review conclusion: under these exact assumptions the contradiction is sound; no shared-OR counterexample was found. Formalization must establish the stated OR-closure/support identities, Rc inclusion and saturation, and Lambda purity in the actual definitions. The numerical case assumptions and any reduction of arbitrary circuits to this case remain separate obligations. This review does not assert an arbitrary cone rewrite, a new normalization theorem, or the full posted lower bound.
