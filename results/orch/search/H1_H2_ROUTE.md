# Conditional H1/H2 construction

This is a sufficient construction target for L-meta-2602, not a circuit or a proof of the posted statement. No 38-gate H1 is known from this work.

Let G be the order-3 incidence function on thirteen point and thirteen line inputs. Seek a legal monotone AND/OR circuit H1 that accepts the 52 incidence pairs and rejects the 702 mixed maximal independent sets. Its values on the two pure-side maximal independent sets are unrestricted. Define H2 as the conjunction of the disjunction of all point inputs and the disjunction of all line inputs. H2 costs 25 gates.

Then H1 AND H2 computes G exactly. A true G assignment contains an incidence pair, so monotonicity forces H1 true and both sides are nonempty. A false G assignment with an empty side is rejected by H2. A false G assignment with both sides nonempty extends to a maximal independent set that is still mixed, so monotonicity and the negative H1 constraint force H1 false.

Every vertex has a nonneighbor. Its singleton therefore extends to a mixed maximal independent set, by first adding a nonneighbor on the other side and then extending the independent set. Consequently H1 rejects every singleton. H2 also rejects every singleton. At the final AND, both operand base values are false at each singleton. The formal derivative recurrence is therefore `(false AND d2) OR (d1 AND false) = false`, for either target direction, regardless of the earlier derivative values. Forward evaluation supplies traces satisfying the posted recurrences. The nonneighbor hypothesis is essential to this singleton argument; it is not a claim about arbitrary bipartite graphs.

A circuit H1 of g <= 38 gates gives total size g + 26 <= 64. The appended H2 output is distinct from the earlier H1 output, and all operands remain earlier wires. If the size is below 63, append AND gates combining the current output with the retained H2 root. These preserve the output because it implies H2; they preserve both zero derivatives because both singleton base values are false; their operands remain distinct. Thus a legal H1 with at most 38 gates would refute the posted 63--64 bound.

The largest monotone function allowed by the H1 negative constraints is G OR Bp OR Bl, where Bp says the selected points meet every line and Bl says the selected lines meet every point. Indeed, an independent mixed assignment extends to a forbidden mixed maximal set. A pure point assignment extends to such a set exactly when at least one line has no selected incident point; the line-side argument is symmetric. This describes the relaxation only; computing it efficiently remains open here.

The accompanying `h1_h2_boundary_check.py` verifies the 704/702 counts, checks every singleton has a mixed maximal extension, and enumerates blocking point sets. Its four-input control uses target pm OR ql, H1 = (p OR l) AND (q OR m), and H2 = (p OR q) AND (l OR m). Their conjunction is an exact seven-gate matching circuit with both target derivatives zero. All sixteen inputs, both mixed maximal negatives, and both derivative directions on each matching edge are checked independently. These finite checks do not supply a plane H1 or establish any plane lower bound.
