# Private leaves force at least one AND selector per point

This is a mathematical argument. It has not been formalized in Lean.

Consider a bipartite edge function on points indexed by $i=1,\ldots,k$. Each point has a line input $z_i$ incident only to that point. Further line inputs $y$ may meet several points. Assume all neighborhoods pairwise intersect in at most one line. This includes compressed projective-plane row modules with one private line per point.

For each subset $S$ of the points, take the independent assignment consisting of the selected points in $S$ and every line disjoint from $S$. It is maximal. Any unselected point can be added only by conflicting with its private line. These are all maximal independent assignments, since every line disjoint from the selected points must be present in a maximal assignment. Write $H_S$ for its complementary minimal vertex cover. It contains $x_i$ exactly when $i\notin S$, it contains $z_i$ exactly when $i\in S$, and it contains any line $y$ exactly when $S$ meets its neighborhood.

Orient every AND gate to one of its two operand positions and keep all OR gates. Starting from the output, the oriented network computes a disjunction of input variables. It majorizes the original function, so its set of input literals is a vertex cover. Given a maximal independent assignment, orient each AND that is false there to a false operand and orient each true AND arbitrarily. The resulting disjunction remains false on that assignment, so its support equals that assignment's complementary minimal cover. Consequently at least $2^k$ distinct orientation supports occur, and the circuit uses at least $k$ AND gates.

Suppose it uses exactly $k$. Every orientation then produces one of the $2^k$ minimal covers and distinct orientations produce distinct covers. Every AND gate is reached from the output under every orientation. Otherwise flipping the choice at an unreached gate would produce the same support. For any wire, its direct OR leaves are the inputs reached by following OR operands and stopping at AND gates. The output support under an orientation is the union of the output's direct OR leaves and the direct OR leaves of the selected operand of every AND gate. For the forward inclusion, trace each reached input from the output and take the last AND on that path. For the reverse inclusion, every AND is reached.

For each input variable $v$, membership in the orientation support is therefore a disjunction of signed orientation bits and possibly a constant. The membership functions of $x_i$ and $z_i$ are nonconstant and complementary. Two nonconstant disjunctions of signed literals that are complementary must be opposite single literals of one bit, after duplicates are removed. Indeed, if the first has two distinct nonopposite literals, every literal of a disjoint disjunction would have to contradict both, which is impossible. All $2^k$ choice patterns of the pairs occur, so different points use different selectors. Relabel the selectors so that $S$ consists of points whose selectors take their $z_i$ side.

The membership function for a shared line is the disjunction of selector bits indexed by its incident points. Its representation by direct OR leaves can have no additional literal. A spurious positive literal would accept its singleton assignment, while a negative literal would accept the zero assignment. Every required positive literal must occur by the singleton assignment test. The same argument for the matched inputs now identifies every AND's direct OR leaf sets. One side contains exactly its point, and the other contains exactly the neighboring lines. No input can appear among direct OR leaves of the output, since there is a minimal cover that omits it.

On an independent assignment, argue through the AND gates in circuit order. All earlier AND values are false by induction. Each operand of the current AND then evaluates as the disjunction of its direct OR leaves. Its point and a neighboring line cannot both be selected, so the current AND is false. Thus every AND is below the final graph function. The project's existing clean-AND lower bound for rectangle-free bipartite graphs gives the row-canonical cost of edges plus $k-1$ gates. Every strict improvement in such a private-leaf module therefore requires at least $k+1$ AND gates.

With extra ANDs, the counting step still gives a reachability constraint. For each minimal cover choose one orientation producing it and let $r_S$ be the number of unreached ANDs. Flipping any subset of those unreached choices preserves reachability and the cover. These $2^{r_S}$-element sets are disjoint for different covers. If the total AND count is $k+b$, then $\sum_S 2^{r_S}\leq 2^{k+b}$, and for uniformly random $S$ one has $\mathbb E[r_S]\leq b$.
