# Common joint gates for saturated singleton carriers

This mathematical argument uses the generic joint-meeting and ineligible-consumer statements from L-meta-971 and the numerical status bounds in the eligible-carrier note. It has not been formalized in Lean.

Let an eligible vertex v have a nonempty set I(v) of ineligible neighbors and at least one eligible neighbor. Its full carrier consists of every wire accepting the singleton v. Every carrier gate can be reached from v through carriers, and the output lies outside. A full-carrier surplus upper bound of |I(v)| leaves at most |I(v)|+1 exit arcs. There is an exit arc at the distinct dedicated consumer h_u for each u in I(v). Each eligible edge vw also has a joint-meeting AND whose two operands separately carry v and w and whose result carries neither. It cannot be a dedicated h_u because the operand opposite the v-carrier at h_u has singleton support {u}. Thus all eligible neighbors meet v at one and the same gate g_v. These arc destinations list every exit, so every physical path from v to the output passes through one of them. Call a vertex satisfying this upper bound saturated.

On a connected component of the graph induced by saturated vertices with eligible edges, all g_v agree with a common AND g. The component's two shores occur on opposite operands of g: a participating vertex belongs to the support of exactly one operand, and each edge joins opposite operands. Consequently g evaluates true on every pair from opposite shores of the component, including its nonedges.

Suppose v,v' belong to the same shore of this component and I(v)=I(v')={u}. After pinning g=1, all remaining paths from either vertex to the output must pass h_u. Write its operands as U,H where U has singleton support {u} and H carries the whole neighborhood N(u). Any path from v or v' to U must have left its full carrier before U, since U carries neither vertex. Its exit cannot be h_u, which follows U, and must therefore pass g. Once g is pinned, U is independent of both variables. On either singleton v or v', H is true by monotonicity and remains true after pinning g=1. Consequently h_u has the same value U on the two assignments {v,w} and {v',w} for any opposite-shore w in the component. Pinning h_u also removes every remaining dependence on the two swapped variables, so the outputs agree. Pinning g itself preserved the original outputs on both assignments because it was already true. Thus v and v' have identical adjacency within the component.

For the first of the three residual six-point type-2 statuses, saturated points 1,2,3,4 meet saturated line 0 on points 1,2, line 2 on points 1,3, and line 1 on points 3,4. They all belong to one component. Points 1 and 4 have the same single ineligible neighbor, line 4. Line 0 is adjacent to point 1 but not to point 4, contradicting the preceding implication. The point permutations from this case to the other two are [1,0,2,3,5,4] and [3,0,4,1,5,2]. They induce the line permutations [0,2,1,4,3,5,7,6,8] and [1,2,0,5,3,4,8,6,7], preserving eligibility, presence and all incidences. Together with the finite census, the degree-two argument, and the separately checked full Fano lower bound for the six-point type-1 restriction, this gives an unrestricted sharp point-star count for every noncollinear point module of PG(2,3) on at most six selected points. The census is a computational diagnostic, and the combination has no Lean derivation.

## A saturated meeting rejects every incident maximal negative

In fact every saturated vertex v with at least two eligible neighbors has a meeting AND whose v-side operand has exactly one same-shore singleton, namely v. Fix an eligible neighbor w. Take the maximal independent test M_w consisting of w and all opposite-shore labels outside N(w). At each of v's exits h_u for an ineligible neighbor u, the carrier-side operand accepts all of N(u), so it is true on M_w: choose a vertex in N(u) outside N(w) by linearity and minimum degree two. If g_v were also true there, pin it to one on both M_w and M_w plus v. On every noncarrier wire the two circuit evaluations would now agree. Indeed they agree on the noncarrier inputs. A noncarrier gate with a carrier input is one of the exits just listed. The pinned g_v already agrees, and each h_u has its carrier input true on both assignments, so compares identically by induction on its other input. At all remaining gates neither parent is a carrier. This gives equal outputs, contradicting that v makes the negative test positive. Thus g_v is false on M_w, while the operand opposite v carries w and is true there. The operand carrying v must reject M_w, so its same-shore singleton labels lie in N(w). Intersecting two distinct eligible neighborhoods leaves just v.

Consequently there is no edge between two saturated vertices that each have at least two eligible neighbors. Their common meeting's v-side operand would contain a second neighbor of the opposite endpoint, contradicting the preceding conclusion. This stronger rule already excludes the first six-point status via point 1 and line 0 and the other cases by the displayed automorphisms.
