# Puncture protection for mixed literal ORs

This supplement shortens the argument for two primitive mixed ORs in fresh_mixed_features.md. It also records a stronger many-feature statement. It is a mathematical observation, not a Lean proof.

Consider an incidence subgraph of a projective plane, or any finite linear bipartite graph. For each flag (p,l), take the negative consisting of the other point neighbors of l and the other line neighbors of p. It is independent by linearity. It need not be maximal for this argument, since an exact circuit rejects it. Among omitted vertices the flag p-l is an isolated component: every other neighbor of either endpoint belongs to the negative. Thus the corresponding component indicator is C_e=P_p+L_l−unit_e, the sum of the two raw star vectors with the common flag counted only once.

Grant any family of freely reusable ORs of raw labels, each containing at least one label of each color. Let A and B be the unions of the point and line labels in these ORs. The monochrome extremes still provide all point-star and line-star columns, since every feature is true on both. If p is outside A and l is outside B, an extra OR false on the flag-puncture negative cannot touch the isolated component with any of its positive flags. Any raw point other than p whose star meets that component must lie on l, and any raw line other than l whose star meets it must pass through p. Those labels all belong to the puncture negative. A feature containing any one of them would be true there and impose no merger. Therefore C_e remains a component column, yielding the unit flag whenever neither endpoint is an anchor.

It follows that every vector in the orthogonal complement of the available rank columns is a circulation supported on the graph retaining only edges at the anchored points or lines. Edges at a remaining degree-one vertex carry zero in any circulation and may be successively pruned. Every nonanchor line remaining must pass through at least two distinct point anchors, and every nonanchor point remaining must lie on at least two distinct line anchors. The rank defect is bounded by the cycle number of this finite graph on the anchors and their pairwise intersections or joins. In particular, with r point anchors and s line anchors the number of potentially cyclic edges is at most rs + r(r−1) + s(s−1), independent of the ambient plane order. The last two terms follow by counting pairs of anchors on each retained nonanchor line or through each retained nonanchor point.

When r,s are at most two, the cycle number is at most one. If neither a line anchor contains both point anchors nor a point anchor lies on both line anchors, the anchor incidences form a matching. The only possible additional pieces are the two-edge path joining the two point anchors through their common line and the two-edge path joining the line anchors through their common point. Connecting these two paths with at most two matching edges produces at most one cycle; omitted pieces cannot increase it. If, for example, a line anchor contains both point anchors, there is no extra path joining these points. The second line anchor has at most one common point with it. That point is either one of the point anchors or the sole nonanchor point that can remain, so the residual graph is a forest. The dual case is identical.

This proves a rank defect of at most one even for arbitrarily many mixed literal ORs using at most two point and two line labels. If those two point anchors lie on neither of those two line anchors, the remaining graph consists only of two disjoint paths and the full flag rank is retained. In particular, apparent long-cycle losses from the star-negative slot bound alone do not survive adding the flag-puncture tests in the four-feature nonincident 2-by-2 arrangement. More general features can accept extra flags disjoint from all their singleton anchors; the puncture argument as written concerns literal ORs.
