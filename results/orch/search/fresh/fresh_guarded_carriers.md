# First-fork and carrier bounds from star negative tests

This is a mathematical extension of the carrier census. It has not been formalized in Lean. Let G be a simple linear bipartite incidence graph with minimum degree at least two, and assume every two vertices of one shore miss at least one vertex of the other shore. Let H be any monotone circuit true on every flag and false on each test M_v consisting of v and all opposite-shore nonneighbors. These hypotheses leave H free on arbitrary additional inputs.

The star tests give exact H(v,Y)=OR over N(v) for every opposite-shore input Y. A positive incidence is sufficient for the forward direction. Every Y disjoint from N(v) is contained in M_v, giving the converse. All singleton tests are false. Two same-shore variables are simultaneously false at the output by choosing an opposite label outside their combined neighborhood and bounding by its M_v. Nonedge mixed pairs are false by a star test. Thus the customary first-fork singleton-purity argument still applies: if a dominating wire of v were already true on another singleton u, take an independent pair consisting of u and a neighbor of v not adjacent to u. Adding v must change the output but cannot change the dominating wire's value. The existence of the pair uses neighborhood incomparability within a shore, and the pure-pair tests on opposite shores.

For a point v, let b be its first physical fork, or the output when there is no fork. Pin b=1 with all points zero, giving a monotone function h1(Y). Let d(Y) be the original b value when only v is set among the points. On every neighboring singleton w, toggling v at this input flips the output from zero to one, hence b must switch from zero to one. It follows that d and h1 both accept each neighbor singleton and therefore dominate f_v=OR N(v). At Y*=complement N(v), the original output with v active is zero by M_v. If h1(Y*)=1 then d(Y*) must be zero; otherwise the output would already be h1(Y*), since all paths from v run through b. In this case d=f_v.

If two distinct same-shore labels share their first fork, h1 dominates both incomparable neighborhood ORs, so h1 equals neither. It follows that d=f_v for both inputs. The same conclusion holds if the stopping wire is the output, since then d is the exact slice f_v. Whenever d=f_v, some physical sibling on v's single-reader initial path computes the exact f_v on the zero-v-shore baseline. To see this, follow the chain from the raw input, which is one on (v,Y*), to b, which is zero there. An AND takes a true input on this path to zero. Its other operand rejects Y*, and as a sibling on the single-reader chain it is independent of v. At every neighboring singleton w, toggling v must propagate from the input all the way through this chain. Every AND sibling therefore accepts w on baseline. By monotonicity, the sibling equals f_v. This proof does not require a zero baseline on arbitrary point-only or line-only assignments.

Thus missing rows have distinct first forks within each shore. Their opposite-shore singleton supports are empty by dominating-wire singleton purity. The use of the actual baseline f_v wire for present rows, the full-carrier counts, joint meetings, terminal conflict marking, and saturated-meeting contradictions in the preceding notes require only the flag and star tests just specified. For example at a maximal-star test M_w, adding an incident v makes a flag and hence changes H. The stronger starred census bounds therefore remain valid for H.

In the nine-point affine plane minimum surplus compatible with the numerical status inequalities is 17, which gives a 37-gate separator lower bound for 36 flags and 21 star negatives. In the seven-point type-3 secant core the corresponding values are 14 and 31 gates for 27 flags and 18 star negatives. The pure-size-two nonblocking premise holds for both. The full seven-point type-3 module has one additional private line. Its removal saves at least one gate because its flag and the singleton point false test certify essentiality. The restricted circuit continues to separate all secant flags and star negatives since each restricted star negative is contained in the corresponding original test. Thus the full module already requires 32 gates for its 28 flags plus 19 star negatives. The eight-point type-2 core and whole order-three plane respectively have the numerical separator lower bounds 35 and 48 gates. These are conditional arithmetic diagnostics combined with the preceding mathematical arguments, not kernel-checked bounds.

If a 33-gate full type-3 module had root AND with a proper operand G true on every flag and every star negative, pin G=1 throughout the other operand and simplify. Both outputs agree on each of these tests, and the root and G disappear, yielding a separator on at most 31 gates. This contradicts the 32-gate floor. In particular neither all-points-nonempty nor all-lines-nonempty may be a root AND operand, even when proper guard subtrees are shared with the other operand. The guard itself must be a proper gate since no one input occurs on every flag.
