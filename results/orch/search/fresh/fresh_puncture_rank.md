# Flag-puncture negatives and lifting eligible forks

This argument is mathematical and unformalized. It gives a formula result and a reduction for circuits with restricted forms of sharing. A rank lower bound for arbitrary circuits does not follow.

For the lifting step, assume a linear bipartite incidence graph of minimum degree at least two and an exact circuit, so the previously established first-fork purity and same-shore neighborhood incomparability hold. In particular each eligible first fork carries exactly its own singleton, and different eligible initial chains are disjoint. For a maximal independent assignment Z, call v in Z externally private if it has a neighbor adjacent to no other selected vertex. For an eligible variable v, its unique initial path all the way to the first fork consists of OR gates: any AND on this path would reject the singleton unless the other operand also carried v, which is impossible because the other operand is independent of v. Every OR sibling is independent of v. An OR sibling is zero on any maximal negative omitting v, because adding v must flip the final output while the already true path gate would mask v. It is also zero on a maximal negative Z containing externally private v: remove v and add its private neighbor w to obtain an independent assignment preserving all other labels in Z. Complete it to a maximal negative omitting v. A sibling that was true on Z remains true there, since it is independent of v, contradicting the first observation.

When all selected labels on a family of negative tests are externally private, remove every eligible input's initial OR chain and reconnect its first fork's readers to the raw input. Accumulate all former OR siblings in an OR at the final output. This reuses one OR gate per removed chain gate. No new internal gate fanout is introduced: each sibling loses its old read and gains one final-OR read. Distinct initial chains are disjoint. On each negative test, all siblings were zero and substituting the smaller raw signals leaves the circuit false. On a positive flag, if a sibling originally true becomes false after rewiring, trace the first changed positive wire in topological order; a removed OR has a true sibling whose value remains true, so the final accumulated OR accepts. If no positive wire changes, the old output remains true. Thus the new circuit separates all original flags from the stated negative tests. Eligible first forks no longer cause sharing of internal gates. In particular, when every internal branch was an eligible first fork of arbitrary outdegree, the resulting circuit is a formula with raw variables usable repeatedly.

In a projective plane of order q≥2, index a negative Z_e by a flag e=(p,l): take all q other points on l and all q other lines through p. The two chosen sets are independent, and every unchosen vertex has a neighbor in the chosen set, so Z_e is maximal. Each chosen point r has a private line through r other than l, since it contains no other chosen point. Dually every chosen line has a private point. Therefore these flag-puncture negatives permit the lifting just described.

Let C be the square matrix on positive flags e' and negative flag labels e with entry one if they share an incident point or an incident line, including e'=e. For any input variable v omitted from Z_e, this column is constant across all positive flags containing v. Indeed v=p or l gives one everywhere on that star; if v is some other point outside l or other line not through p, the entries are zero everywhere on its star. This is the component property for the isolated flag (p,l) in the vertex-cover complement of Z_e. Hence every admissible Karchmer-Wigderson input leaf rectangle of C has column vectors constant on its row set.

Let B be the unsigned vertex-by-flag incidence matrix. Then C=BᵀB−I. Writing N=q²+q+1 and d=q+1, the symmetric point-line adjacency has eigenvalues ±d and ±√q because the point-line incidence matrix M satisfies MMᵀ=qI+J. Consequently BBᵀ=dI+Adj has eigenvalues 0,2d,d±√q. None equals one for q≥2. The nonzero spectra of BBᵀ and BᵀB coincide, so C is invertible and has rank Nd.

The standard augmented-column-rank proof for monotone formulas gives at least Nd−1 OR gates from these tests alone. Add the constant column to the restricted matrix at each formula subproblem; legal input leaves have rank at most one. An OR splits the row set, costing at most one plus the sum of the children’s rank-minus-one potentials, and an AND splits columns with a common constant column, costing at most the sum of the children’s potentials. The root potential is Nd−1. In particular a circuit whose only internal branching wires are eligible first forks has this OR count after lifting.

For unrestricted circuits, the witnesses still allow inexpensive separators based on other forms of shared computation. The argument gives neither a general gate bound nor an unrestricted projective-plane solution.
