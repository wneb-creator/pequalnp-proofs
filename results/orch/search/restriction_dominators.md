# Private degree-two line cores and missing neighborhood ORs

These are mathematical arguments for finite monotone circuits. They have not been formalized in Lean. The full projective-plane bound remains open in this attempt.

Use a strict live binary AND/OR DAG on all essential point and line variables. Pruning constants, repeated operands and dead wires only shrinks a circuit. For a nonoutput wire its surplus is its outdegree minus one. The total surplus equals the gate count plus one minus the number of inputs.

Write `B(w)` for the set of point labels `i` for which `w` accepts the input containing only `x_i`. Write `f_j` for the OR of the point neighbors of line `j`. A row is present if some wire equals `f_j` on the entire subcube with all lines zero. Other rows are missing.

## A missing row consumes a branching wire

Starting at `y_j`, walk toward the output while there is one consumer. Stop at the first wire with at least two consumers, or at the output. Call the stopping wire `b`. Because the path taken so far was unique, `b` dominates every path from `y_j` to the output.

Set all other line inputs zero and leave the points `X` free. The output depends on `y_j` only through `b`. With all points one, changing `y_j` from zero to one flips the output, so the baseline value of `b` is zero even at all points one. Monotonicity gives `b(X,0)=0` on the entire baseline. Independently pin `b=1`, set all lines zero and call the resulting output `h(X)`. Pinning `b=0` gives zero. Thus

`f_j(X) = b(X,e_j) AND h(X)`.

The OR of a nonempty point set `S` has a unique maximal false point assignment, the complement of `S`. If this OR equals a meet of finitely many monotone functions dominating it, one of the functions must equal the OR, by evaluating at that maximal false assignment. If `b` is the same first branching wire for two lines with incomparable neighborhoods, `h` dominates both neighborhood ORs, so differs from both and `b(X,e_j)=f_j(X)` for both. If `b` is the output, the same conclusion holds directly.

Every sibling on the unique initial path is independent of `y_j`. With all points one, all OR siblings must be zero for the path to carry the `y_j` flip. Consequently they are identically zero on the baseline. The remaining AND siblings multiply, giving `b(X,e_j)` as the meet of their baseline point functions. If that meet equals `f_j`, a sibling is an actual wire equal to `f_j` on the baseline. In particular, in any family of pairwise incomparable missing rows, the first branching wires exist, are distinct, have surplus at least one each, and have empty point support `B(b)`.

## Point wires with a connected singleton-support path

For each point `i`, let `R_i` consist of wires reachable from `x_i` along a directed path, including its endpoints, on which every wire has `B(w)={i}`. These sets are disjoint. The reachability condition is necessary: an AND of two multipoint-support wires may recover exactly the singleton support `{i}`.

The input `x_i` is the unique raw input of `R_i`, and every other member has at least one parent in `R_i`. The output has empty point support. Writing `E_i` for the number of arcs from `R_i` to its complement, the sum of outdegrees on `R_i` counts those exits plus at least `|R_i|-1` internal arcs. Hence `surplus(R_i) ≥ E_i-1`.

For a present row `j` and `i` in its neighborhood `S` of size at least two, trace back from its baseline wire toward `x_i`. Evaluate each wire on the singleton of `i` and on the complement of `S`, with all lines zero. Start with values `(1,0)`. At an OR choose a child that is one on the singleton. At an AND choose a child that is zero on the complement. The other required value holds automatically. The path terminates at `x_i`, the only input with this pair of values. Every wire on it contains `i` in its point support and has support contained in `S`. In the forward direction, the first wire whose support has size at least two gives an exit from `R_i` to a support of size at least two contained in `S`. Distinct rows of a linear incidence system, meaning rows with at most one shared point, give distinct exits.

For another row `l` incident to `i`, use the positive assignment `(x_i,y_l)` and the negative assignment consisting of `y_l` and all points outside `N(l)`. The same backward trace from the output again terminates at `x_i`: `y_l` belongs to both assignments, and only `x_i` belongs to the positive alone. The forward path exits `R_i`, and the target's point support is contained in `N(l)`. It cannot equal `{i}`, since a pure `{i}` successor of an `R_i` vertex also belongs to `R_i`. The target might have singleton support different from `{i}`, which is harmless: it cannot be any of the exits to a multipoint support for a different present row. When `N(l)={i}` the target must have empty point support.

## Exact private degree-two core

Let there be `k` point centers, with at least one private degree-one line per center. Suppose every nonprivate line has degree two and no two of them have the same endpoints. Let `s` count nonprivate lines and `t` count the present nonprivate lines. For every center, its private line supplies one extra exit beyond one exit per incident present nonprivate line. Summing the pure-carrier surplus bounds therefore gives at least `2t`. The `s-t` missing nonprivate lines have incomparable neighborhoods, and their first branching wires have empty point support, so contribute `s-t` further surplus disjoint from those carriers. The total surplus is at least `s+t`, hence at least `s`.

If the number of private lines is `a`, the graph has `k+a+s` input variables and `a+2s` edges. Its point-star circuit has `a+2s+k-1` gates, exactly `inputs-1+s`. The unrestricted lower bound just proved matches it. A `k`-point arc with a tangent per point gives this type of core on restriction. The full conic in an odd-prime coordinate plane has `k=q+1`, one tangent per center, and `s=binom(k,2)`.

## Every linear incidence system with line degree at most two

The previous statement extends to the case where some or all private tangents are absent. Discard isolated inputs and assume the incidence graph has at least one edge. Form the simple graph `H` of degree-two lines on the point centers. Include all point centers as vertices of `H`, including centers attached only to private lines. Let `h` count connected components of `H` that are trees and have no private line anywhere in the component. Write `D` for the centers with neither a private line nor an incident missing degree-two line.

For every point center outside `D`, get one additional exit beyond its incident present degree-two lines, using a private line or the two-assignment path for an incident missing line. The pure-carrier estimate is then `2t-|D|`. Distinct missing degree-two line first forks supply another `s-t` units. The resulting surplus is at least `s+t-|D|`.

Each connected component of `H[D]` with a neighbor outside `D` has at least `v-1` internal present edges and at least one present edge to the outside, where `v` is its number of vertices. All these edges are distinct across components of `H[D]`. Each component closed in `H` has no private line and has all its edges present. A closed component has at least `v` edges unless it is a tree, when it has `v-1`. Thus `t≥|D|-h` and the total surplus is at least `s-h`.

If `k` is the number of centers and `a` the number of private lines, the size lower bound is `k+a+2s-h-1`. There is a cover of size `k-h`: take all centers in each component other than the `h` trees and take all degree-two line vertices in each of those trees. This cover is minimum. In a component with a private line, match the root center to that line and each other center to the line of its parent edge in a spanning tree. In a component with a cycle, match each cycle vertex to the line of its following edge and extend with a spanning forest rooted at the cycle. In one of the `h` trees, matching each nonroot center to its parent-edge line saturates the line side. These disjoint matchings have total size `k-h`. In each component the cover chosen is one whole side, so its stars partition the incident edge set. The corresponding circuit has `|E|+k-h-1=k+a+2s-h-1` gates. This proves the exact unrestricted bound `|E|+minimum vertex cover size-1` throughout this class, including the incidence graphs obtained by subdividing the edges of any simple graph once. The observation that a minimum center degree of two suffices without private lines is due to the fresh-math strategy.

## Missing rows in a full plane

For a plane of order `q`, write `d=q+1`, `N=q²+q+1`, and let `M` be the missing lines, `m=|M|`, `U` their union. Give each point one exit for every incident present line, and one more exit if it belongs to `U`, using any incident missing line. The root-connected pure carriers give at least `d(N-m)+|U|-N` total surplus. The distinct first branching wires of all missing rows give `m` more. Thus

`gates ≥ N(q+2)-1 - qm + |U|`.

One may also interchange the two colors. The formula proves the target when `|U|≥qm`, including all `m≤3` and missing lines contained in a pencil. A pencil of `m>0` lines covers `qm+1` points. When `m=0`, the point-carrier exits alone give exactly the desired lower bound. The expression loses information when many missing lines have different intersections. Neither the identity for the sum of locally restricted surpluses nor a compensating charge for this loss has been established.

## Combining both colors into an unconditional plane bound

Write `T` for the total surplus, `m_L` and `m_P` for the missing row counts after baselining in opposite directions. Within each color the missing labels have distinct first branching wires. Let `b` be the number of first branching wires shared between the two colors. Such a wire has empty singleton support on both colors: each color's first-fork argument forces it to be false even on the all-inputs-true assignment of the other color, with its own color zero. Thus a shared-fork label is ineligible in the sense of the generic eligible-cover theorem from L-meta-971. Indeed the first fork does not accept its singleton, there are no forks earlier on its initial path, and there is no downstream singleton carrier. To see the last assertion, pinning the first fork to zero makes every input-dependent path to a downstream wire insensitive to that label. A singleton carrier must change from the all-zero evaluation. Other gates depending on that label before this first fork are also confined to its unique initial path.

The eligible-cover theorem says that the ineligible labels form an independent set. Therefore none of the `b` shared points lies on any of the `b` shared lines. If `r_x` counts those lines through `x`, then `sum(r_x)=(q+1)b` and `sum(r_x²)=b(b+q)`, by unique intersection of two distinct lines and double counting. There are at most `N-b` nonzero summands. Cauchy's inequality gives `(q+1)² b ≤ (N-b)(b+q)` for `b>0`, or equivalently `(b+q)² ≤ q(q+1)²`. Consequently `b≤B` with `B=floor((q+1)sqrt(q))-q`. The integer bound also holds for `b=0`.

The missing forks supply `T≥m_L+m_P-B`. The one-color inequality and regularity give `T≥qN-(q-1)m_L` and `T≥qN-(q-1)m_P`, since any `m` lines cover at least `m` points by incidence counting. Adding those inequalities and applying `m_L+m_P≤T+B` proves

`(q+1)T ≥ 2qN-(q-1)B`.

The resulting unconditional gate lower bound is `2N-1+ceil((2qN-(q-1)B)/(q+1))`. It is `4N-O(q^(3/2))` as `q` grows. It gives 43 gates for order three and 108 gates for order five. The sharper maximum of the separate inequalities can be used for specific missing sets. This estimate still falls short of the unrestricted `N(q+2)-1` target.

## Counting mandatory exits to ineligible neighbors

Here use full singleton supports, on both colors together. Let `I` be the set of ineligible vertices and `A` its complement. For every `v` in `A` reserve one surplus unit at its first branching wire carrying `v`. This is the first physical fork on its unique initial path. Indeed a first fork rejecting the singleton would dominate every downstream occurrence of the input and prevent later singleton carrier wires. The reserved wire has full support exactly `{v}`, by the pure-branch lemma in L-meta-971. Write `R` for all positive-support surplus minus these `|A|` reserved units. Keep all empty-support surplus separate.

For an ineligible vertex `u`, the theorem `sem_inelig_consumer` in the generic L-meta-971 argument gives a unique consuming AND `h_u` with full support empty. One operand has support exactly `{u}`, and the opposite operand carries every neighbor of `u`. Different ineligible vertices have different consumers: if two used the same gate, equal operand positions would equate the two labels, and opposite positions would force a singleton operand to contain the other vertex's entire neighborhood, of degree at least two.

For a vertex `v`, let `k` be the number of ineligible neighbors. Each neighbor `u` in `I` gives a distinct exit from the full `v`-carrier set into `h_u`. If `v` also has an eligible neighbor `z`, there is a further exit at a joint-meeting AND for the edge `vz` (the theorem `sem_joint_meeting`). It cannot coincide with any `h_u`, since its operand opposite the `v` carrier must carry `z`, whereas the corresponding operand of `h_u` has singleton support `{u}`. When `k≥2` and every vertex has degree at least three, this supplies at least three exits. The vertex `v` is eligible because `I` is independent.

Mark the following outgoing arcs of positive-support branching wires. At every nonreserved such wire, mark all of them. At a reserved first fork of outdegree at least three, mark all except one. Mark no arcs at a reserved first fork of outdegree two. This marks at most `2R` arcs: an unreserved wire of outdegree `d≥2` costs `d-1` units and marks `d≤2(d-1)` arcs, and a reserved wire of outdegree `d≥3` costs `d-2` residual units and marks `d-1≤2(d-2)` arcs.

Fix `v` with at least two ineligible neighbors. Every full carrier of `v` is reachable from its input along `v`-carrying wires by the singleton-support recursion. The input path before its first branching wire is unique, and every subsequent branching wire reached while still carrying `v` is unreserved (a reserved pure first fork could only belong to `v` itself). At the first fork there is at most one unmarked arm if its outdegree exceeds two. When its outdegree equals two, both initial arms are unmarked, but at least one must encounter a further branching wire while carrying `v`, since there are at least three different exits overall. The path on that arm is unique before this further fork, and every outgoing arc there is marked. Thus at most one of the distinct `h_u` exits has a `v`-carrier path free of marked arcs.

Each `h_u` reached by a marked carrier path is determined by the last marked arc on that path. From that arc forward there is a unique physical continuation through all intermediate wires up to `h_u`: every intermediate wire carries `v`, none is the already-passed first fork of `v`, and any intervening branch would have all outgoing arcs marked. The first full-empty-support wire on this continuation is `h_u`. The terminal designation therefore depends only on the marked arc itself and works simultaneously for all carrier labels. Let `J` consist of the ineligible vertices whose distinct consumers are designated this way. We have `|J|≤2R`, and every `v` with at least two ineligible neighbors has at most one neighbor in `I` outside `J`.

For a missing ineligible line `j`, its first fork has empty full singleton support. The missing-row argument already excludes every point singleton, and ineligibility excludes its own line singleton. To exclude another line singleton `k`, let `h(X)` be the output with the fork pinned to one and every line input zero. The earlier factorization gives `f_j≤h`. If the fork accepted singleton `k`, then it would equal one on every assignment `(X,e_k)`; monotonicity of the pinned circuit would give `h≤f_k`. This contradicts incomparability of the distinct line neighborhoods. The dual argument applies to missing ineligible points.

For a full plane, two distinct points outside `J` and in `I` have a common line and would violate the preceding property. Two distinct lines are ruled out by their common point. Hence `2R≥|I|-2`. If the counts of present rows in the two colors are `t_P,t_L`, the empty-support surplus is at least the maximum of the two counts of missing ineligible labels (distinct first forks within each color). This maximum is at least `(|I|-t_P-t_L)/2`. Combining with the positive-support reservation proves

`T ≥ 2N-1-(t_P+t_L)/2`.

The one-color bound already gives `T≥N+(q-1)t_P` and `T≥N+(q-1)t_L`. Consequently `qT≥(2q-1)N-(q-1)=2q³+q²`. In particular the unrestricted gate count is at least `4q²+3q+1`, or `4N-q-3`. This gives 46 gates at order three and 116 at order five. This stronger argument is mathematical and has not undergone the repository's Lean check.

Integral incidence counting improves the last gate bound to `4N-q-2`. Suppose `T≤2N-q-2`. The Hall bounds give `t≤q+1` for either color count. If `t=q+1`, at most one center can have all its incident rows present (two distinct centers have incident-row union of size `2q+1`). Thus the one-color exit count gives `T≥N+q(q+1)-1=2N-2`, contradicting the cap. When `t≤q`, every center has a missing neighbor, and the exact one-color bound gives `T≥N+qt`. Consequently `t≤q-1` under the putative cap. The two-color marked-arc bound would then imply `T≥2N-q`, again a contradiction. Hence `T≥2N-q-1`. The resulting lower bounds are 47 gates at order three and 117 at order five.

For order three, equality at 47 gates is itself impossible. It would give `T=22`. The same one-color counts force at most three present labels on either side, and `T≥25-(t_P+t_L)/2` then forces exactly three on each. Equality in its derivation requires every present label to be ineligible and the two numbers of missing ineligible labels to agree. The equal-sized ineligible sets have size at most three on each side, by the independent balanced set bound above. Thus each ineligible set is precisely the three present labels, there is no empty-support surplus, and the 20 eligible first forks leave `R=2`.

Each point `i` incident to `k_i>0` present lines has at least `k_i` units in its connected pure-point region (it has at least one missing neighbor). At most one of those is the reserved eligible unit of `i`; no other eligible label's pure first fork can belong to this region. Thus the required residual sum is at least `12-|union of the three lines|`. Three nonconcurrent lines have union of size nine and would require three residual units. The lines must be concurrent at `p`, and both residual units must belong to `R_p`. Dually the three present points are collinear on a line `l`, and both residual units belong to the corresponding pure-line region of `l`. Since the three selected points and three selected lines are mutually nonincident, `p` is the fourth point on `l`: the lines missing the three selected points are exactly the three other lines through this fourth point. In particular a positive-support branching wire carrying singleton `p` and singleton `l` exists, since the two residual units are each in both required regions.

Each of `p` and `l` has its three ineligible neighbors and just one eligible neighbor, namely the other endpoint. Its full singleton carrier has at most three surplus units (its own reserved unit and the two residual units). The full carrier is root-connected, so it has at most four exits. It has exactly four, the three distinct ineligible consumers and the joint-meeting AND for the edge `pl`. The latter is common to the two exit lists and its operands carry the two endpoints separately, each excluding the other. From the wire carrying both endpoints follow any directed path to the output, whose support is empty. The first loss of either label cannot occur at an ineligible consumer: its output is empty, forcing both carrier labels to exit, whereas the two lists of ineligible consumers are disjoint. It cannot occur at the joint meeting since neither operand carries both labels. This contradiction proves at least 48 gates for the order-three projective plane, still below its 64-gate canonical circuit.

## Affine plane of order three

The 21-input affine module has nine points, twelve lines, and 36 edges. At the proposed 43-gate search threshold `T=23`. For `t_L` present line neighborhoods, let `D_P` count points with all four incident rows present. The corresponding exact one-color carrier/fork lower bound is `T≥12+2t_L-D_P`. Dually `T≥9+3t_P-D_L`. Enumerating the 4096 subsets of lines gives, for `t_L=0,..,6`, respectively `1,12,66,220,495,792,252` admissible presence masks, and excludes every larger count. At `t_L=6` its six lines must contain the full pencil of four lines through a unique point, and the other two may be any of the remaining eight. Enumerating the 512 point subsets gives `1,9,36,84,126,126` admissible masks at `t_P=0,..,5`, and excludes larger counts. A wire equals a neighborhood OR on the opposite-color-zero subcube precisely when it accepts each neighbor singleton and rejects the complement of the neighborhood on that same subcube.

For the affine module the terminal charging also shows `2R≥|I|-3`. Unmarked ineligible points number at most one. Any two unmarked ineligible lines must be parallel. A full three-line parallel class of ineligible lines prevents every point from being ineligible, so at most three total ineligibles remain unmarked.
