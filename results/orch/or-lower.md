## Output-sensitive incidence extraction (03:17–03:20 UTC)

The finite incidence-only graph observation is useful but is not itself a circuit invariant. If a graph H contained in the 52 incidence edges induces a connected graph on every generic negative row's 19 zero variables, H must have at least51 edges. A circuit-to-H extraction with |H| <= OR_count would finish the goal, but constructing it is the hard step.

A universal extraction theorem for arbitrary bipartite edge functions is false. For a matching with m disjoint edges, take a maximal false assignment selecting exactly one endpoint of each edge. Its critical zero set consists of the other endpoints and induces an edgeless subgraph of the matching. Yet the edge function has the standard circuit with m−1 OR gates. Thus the extraction must exploit the order-3 plane and the restricted generic rows, not merely monotonicity, pair correctness, and local sensitivity.

The ordinary star circuit also prevents literal labeling: its39 lower OR gates combine same-side point or line variables, whereas the extracted links must be mixed incidence edges. Their appropriate labels depend on a later AND context. A possible accounting scheme carries incidence-link sets with each wire, allowing one unpaid link for each active AND/star component. Top OR gates merge components and pay debts. In the standard circuit,13 star components have13 free anchor links, and12 top OR gates pay all but one. This explains the arithmetic but does not justify arbitrary nested AND gates: AND filtering may retain some positive flags while discarding other flags and false inputs. There is no proved rule preserving a single global unpaid-link budget across that operation.

An alternative formulation would assign every pair of incidence edges a generic-row cut whose only crossing incidence links are that pair. To obtain51 OR gates one must show each OR gate can be charged only pairs incident to one fixed flag (a star in the complete graph on52 flags). No such output-sensitive charging lemma has been established. Allowing arbitrary cuts per gate immediately falls back to the weak signature bound.

Status: no sound full extraction proof. This is a substantive obstruction, not a completed reduction hypothesis. The checked signature lemmas remain available independently.

## Full-bound exploration after 21 preservation

The exact global51 bound remains open. Fixing one point input p to true and all other point inputs to false yields a line-input function accepting its four-line pencil Pp and rejecting all affine caps outside Pp. Nonlinear terms consisting of three concurrent outside lines remain permitted. Consequently, `(S OR a) AND (S OR b) AND (S OR c) = S OR abc` is valid cleanup when a,b,c are concurrent outside S=Pp; replacing all cleanup by pure unions is unsound.

A restricted architecture appears to admit a39-OR lower bound: internal gates are pure unions of line inputs, and each of13 outputs is a free intersection of available unions. If targetp has no exact-pencil union node, classify useful guards as private (their singleton support contains only pencilp) or shared (at least two pencils). Let a,b count these. For a<=2, select one extra line from each private guard and one other pointq from each shared guard. The generic four-cap negatives force the q values to vertex-cover a complete four-partite graph: part sizes are (3,3,3,3) with zero selected lines; (2,2,2,2) with one; and (2,1,1,1) with two distinct selected lines. The vertex-cover minima are9,6,3, so3a+b>=9. Three private guards already satisfy this inequality. Any useful guard is false on a four-cap, so its support contains at most three pencils. Assigning credit1 to a private guard and1/3 per shared target gives at least3 per target and at most1 per guard.
