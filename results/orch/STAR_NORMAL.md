# Star normalization route

A single-level flattening is not routine for quadratic monotone functions: the general conjecture is false (Jukna, Disproving the Single Level Conjecture, primary paper https://web.vu.lt/mif/s.jukna/ftp/single1.pdf, read 2026-11-25). Those counterexamples use saturated graphs and do not refute our triangle/C4-free case.

Current local exchange: an AND gate can be replaced by a unary OR reading one child whenever that child already implies the complete circuit output. This preserves the function and gate count. A subsequent copy-deletion would strictly shrink the circuit, so minimum circuits cannot contain this pattern. Proof in lemmas/StarNormalForm.lean adapts the proven monotonicity core from L_meta_2772, generalized to replace a complete gate. Compiled successfully with lake env lean, now 456 lines after adding deletion and the strict minimality consequence. Complete latest stdout/stderr is recorded in lemmas/StarNormalForm.validation.txt.

Main unresolved difficulty: a dirty AND gate owning disjoint edges may require multiple downstream filtering AND gates. Replacing it by a child or OR can introduce nonedge outputs. Need global exchange or charging argument; no established normalization yet.

Checked strict consequence: `StarNormal.CopyDeletion.least_no_and_operand_below` excludes any AND operand that already implies the full output in a minimum ReadsBack monotone circuit. It requires no graph restrictions. This rules out some reducible structures but does not handle a genuinely dirty AND whose operands do not imply the output.

Second checked helper: StarSandwich.lean proves that any pointwise-downward monotone circuit rewrite preserving every positive edge-pair test preserves the complete graph function. This removes all larger-input obligations from a downward local exchange. Both helper files are stable.

Critical-edge local exchange now checked in CriticalRestriction.lean: an AND with operands a,b can restrict a to input c without changing size or graphFun, provided c implies a and every edge lost when the gate is forced false contains c. Nonpivotal edges survive by monotonicity above false-substitution; pivotal edges keep the gate true. The resulting gate is true only on pairs containing c, hence owns a star. Earlier gates remain unchanged, though descendant ownership may change; global progress should use maximal initial good-gate prefix, not number of bad gates. The missing bridge is deriving a common center for pivotal edges of a first bad owning AND, or finding enough private support gates to pay for a multigate exchange.

Private-support obstruction checked in PrivateSupportLimitation.lean: an eight-vertex triangle/C4-free graph has a 15-gate TopForm circuit whose first AND owns and is pivotal on two disjoint edges; each operand OR also feeds a useful clean star AND. Thus even TopForm + first bad AND + disjoint pivotal edges does not force a private operand OR without invoking minimality. Exhaustive finite Lean checks certify exact graph function, model, graph restrictions, ownership, and sharing. Its 7 AND + 8 OR gates on a 10-edge graph also refute the unrestricted OR-count bound |E|-1. A 13-gate star-cover circuit exists, so this does not refute the posted minimum-circuit statement.

The private-support example admits a size-preserving local repair despite having no private operand ORs: retain all four shared OR support wires and replace the three-AND matching block by two edge ANDs and their OR, using the same three gates. This suggests charging replacement cost to the entire interacting junk block, rather than to one private support gate. A P4 three-clause block instead needs one extra gate, supplied by the middle-edge OR clause; triangle-freeness prevents that OR from feeding another clean star. General OR sharing through other dirty gates remains the unresolved case.

TwoLeafRepair.lean is checked: if a gate accepts two singleton inputs and every pivotal graph edge touches one of those vertices, replacing the gate by the OR of those two inputs preserves graphFun and size, and removes its AND ownership. This needs no ReadsBack and allows equal singleton vertices. It handles a real singleton-positive repair case, including some disjoint pivotal-edge families. The remaining case includes a singleton-positive bad gate pivotal on an edge disjoint from all singleton-positive inputs, and singleton-silent dirty rectangles with disjoint pivotal edges.

Concrete multigate repair now checked in MatchingExchange.lean: if g=(a OR c) AND (b OR d), h=(a OR d) AND (b OR c), and their only reader is t=g AND h, change the three gates to g=a AND b, h=c AND d, t=g OR h. Arbitrary external readers of the four support ORs remain allowed. The proof establishes Monotone2, exact full-circuit function, same size, and star pair acceptance for both repaired ANDs. It requires that neither g nor h is the circuit output. The core permits arbitrary gate semantics elsewhere and does not require ReadsBack. MatchingExchangeCore.lean preserves the first compiled version. The global problem remains proving a first bad gate falls into a repairable component; arbitrary dirty readers are not covered.

Prefix-progress qualification: first normalize to a backward-reading minimum using BackwardNormalize. Earlier syntactic cones need not be preserved under later-gate changes in an arbitrary circuit with forward references.

ExternalRepair.lean now checks a stronger three-gate repair allowing arbitrary outside readers of the two changed AND gates. New g/h need only imply graphFun and jointly cover every graph-edge pair accepted at either old g/h. Replace their common AND reader t by OR, and require t to imply the new circuit output. On graph-false inputs all three changed gates are false, so monotone gate simulation proves soundness. On positive edge pairs either the old gates are inactive (reverse simulation preserves acceptance), or the new top rescues them. Individual lower bounds g-new <= g-old and h-new <= h-old are unnecessary. Geometry and library agents are supplying the concrete four-vertex candidates and preserved OR-path certificate.
