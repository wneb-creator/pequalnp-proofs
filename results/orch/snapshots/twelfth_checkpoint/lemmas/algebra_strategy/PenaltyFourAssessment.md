The higher-penalty cases remain open. No result here proves three junk gates are necessary or refutes L-meta-650.

Write `P = 2 * centreless.card + junk.card + (orN \ lam).card` and `U = Ustar`. The generic checked cover lemmas give two useful necessary inequalities when combined with the established TopForm count for this 52-edge, degree-four graph:

- Extending a cover after deleting `U` costs at most `|U|` vertices. Thus `P + 1 ≤ 2 * |U|` for a circuit of size at most 63.
- A cover `K` after deletion satisfies `52 ≤ |U| + 4 * |K|`. Thus `4 * P + 4 ≤ 5 * |U|`.

The graph-specific corollaries are in `PlaneResidualCounting.lean`; its validation file and complete build log record whether that assembled version has finished checking. In particular, penalty four requires at least four residual edges. Equality at four forces circuit size 63, twelve centreful tops, and every minimum cover of the residual graph to have size twelve.

The replayable finite geometry certificate checks all 8192 point-subset closures. Every independent set meeting both parts has size at most ten; the only independent twelve-sets are twelve points or twelve lines. If four edges are deleted and twelve vertices cover the remainder, degree counting is tight, so those twelve vertices are independent. Consequently the four deleted edges are exactly the star of the omitted point or line. This last star classification is computational geometry, not yet a Lean theorem.

L-meta-6240 adds a structural consequence: if every gate in `centreless ∪ junk ∪ mixing` were active on at most one residual edge, it would imply `2 * |U| ≤ P`, contradicting the first inequality. Hence some such gate must be active on at least two residual edges. This is not an upper bound on the number of residual edges.

For exactly two junk gates and penalty four, the remaining numerical cases are `(centreless.card, nonlam.card) = (1,0)` or `(0,2)`. Raw-star equality does not identify the complete Boolean functions of the tops. Their operands can also receive junk outputs through OR paths, and those extra signals can cover residual edges. The accepted L-meta-8097 theorem concerns raw-side unions in an AndCount circuit computing its entire graph; it does not provide a subcircuit computing the residual graph from the raw cross pairs of these tops.

The `(1,0)` case suggests a possible replacement argument: zero nonlam ORs should isolate an AND-only exceptional cone consisting of the two junk gates and the centreless top. Covering all four edges of the missing star then requires a pure-OR clause containing its four neighbor lines. At least three joins of those lines cannot be shared with another full raw row star, by the absence of four-cycles. Replacing the exceptional cone by an ordinary star could save gates. A proof must still extract that cone, account for shared nodes, and preserve the complete output function. None of those steps is silently assumed here.

The geometric steps also now have generic Lean proofs: `TightCoverGeometry.lean` proves that equality in the degree-cover count makes the cover independent and that independent twelve-sets in the stated bipartite system are pure; `TightCoverStar.lean` identifies the four deleted edges as the missing vertex star. The concrete plane adapter is tracked separately in `PlanePenaltyFourStar.lean` and its build receipt.

For the `(0,2)` case, the proposed remaining extraction can be stated precisely. An affected point wing of a top centered at `q` needs a nonlam OR whose nonempty raw support lies in `{q}`; different centers cannot share that OR. An affected line wing has raw support contained in `N(q)`, so it cannot share a point-wing OR. With only two nonlam ORs, either two different point wings are affected and all line wings are pure (covering at most two edges of the missing star), or one top must contain both exceptional wings. The latter should isolate the two-owner function of its center and the missing star center.

After fixing all outside points and all lines outside that double pencil to false, every fixed outside row's pure-OR prefix reduces to false, a raw alias, or one of the nine cross-pair OR features covered by `FreeCrossPairLower.lean`. An eight-paid-gate exceptional cone would therefore contradict the checked pair-gadget lower bound. What remains unproved is the extraction itself: the first-mixing-OR assignment, the uniqueness of its raw support, the absence of other junk routes, and the exact count of gates shared with ordinary tops. The numerical equality and raw-star classification alone do not establish these facts.
