# A five-point patch with four collinear points

In the 64-gate all-point-star circuit, five complete point stars and their four combining ORs cost 24 gates. Pick four collinear points and one point outside their common line. Keep that line and the four lines joining the outside point to each of the four collinear points. The retained incidence graph consists of four internally disjoint paths of length three with the same endpoints. It has ten vertices and twelve edges. The eight unretained incident lines are private to chosen points. An unselected point is incident to at most one retained line, since every pair of retained lines meets at a chosen point. Hence every outside-star OR becomes a raw literal or zero on this restriction.

A proposed 23-gate replacement would leave at most 15 gates for the four-path graph after the eight private-line deletions. The finite calculation here excludes 15. A direct construction uses 16 gates. This result concerns a five-star patch with exterior pure-line ORs, under the same assumptions as `fresh_meta3773_K4_FREE_OR.md`. The private-line restriction and finite search have not been transferred to Lean.

## Finite search and graph bound

The ten raw vertex literals are mandatory. Fifteen binary gates allow at most six excess reads. The semantic search uses the twelve edge pairs for positive tests and the seventeen maximal independent assignments for negative tests. It enumerates multisets of repeated primitive occurrences and splits the earliest shared internal gate in exactly the manner described in the K₄ patch record. The numbers of initial orbits, by primitive repeat count zero through six, are 1, 4, 13, 36, 92, 212 and 459. None of the 817 orbits passes. A positive test recognizes the direct 16-gate upper bound. The extended kernel uses 64-bit truth vectors and supports the 17 negative rows; 96 partial-specification tests compare it against the Python read-once recurrence.

One additional necessary condition speeds the search. The graph has degree at least two, no four-cycle and no twins. For every edge, `covers_edge` from `lean/PEqualNP/Outside/L_meta_615.lean` gives an eligible endpoint, one whose singleton signal reaches a branching wire. The off-edge-neighbor hypothesis follows from degree at least two and absence of four-cycles. `branch_injective` maps distinct eligible vertices injectively to their first branching wires. Let R be the vertices whose input literal is read repeatedly. If an eligible vertex is outside R, its first branching wire is internal, since a raw input can carry only its own singleton. Hence at least the vertex cover number of G−R distinct internal branching wires are necessary. The root search prunes a primitive multiset only when the remaining internal excess budget is below that number. Independent expansion of any subsequently considered free OR inputs can enlarge R by all their supports and count repeated free outputs as possible internal branches. There are no free inputs in this four-path instance.

Independent finite incidence classification checks all 1,287 five-point sets and all their outside pure-OR signatures. The 117 sets with a four-point line have the geometry in this note. There are also 702 sets with two three-point lines and 468 with one three-point line. Those two models have respectively 14 and 17 core edges. Their outcomes are separate from the four-path calculation.

To reproduce the four-path computation in an empty scratch directory, copy only the source and model JSON, compile `fresh_meta3773_readonce_ext.cpp` as `fresh-meta3773-readonce-ext.so` with `g++ -std=c++17 -O3 -shared -fPIC`, and run `python fresh_meta3773_five_search.py 2 0 j` for j from zero through six. Each run writes an atomic per-orbit progress JSON and may be resumed. The saved searches were split into short wall-clock chunks, some of which timed out and then resumed from the last fully decided orbit. All seven final progress reports show complete coverage.
