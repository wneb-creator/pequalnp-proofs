# Single-row chart with a finite choice of diagonal entry

This chart covers every prospective three-space having at least one surjective row-difference map. Permute that row to index zero. Its three off-diagonal entry maps are then also independent, by the row-kernel argument in fresh_diagonal_anchor.md. The unique A in J+L with those three off-diagonals zero is diag(λ,S), where λ is a root of X⁴−16X³+72X²−96X+24. Define R_S(w) as the sum, for 0≤k≤3, of the coefficient of jᵏ in per(S+jJ₃) times wᵏ/k!. Then R_S(w) is the product of the three factors w+μ indexed by the other three roots. These constraints fix the lower-block entry sum, the sum of all its 2×2 permanents, and its permanent.

For any prescribed three lower-block off-diagonal entries of S, its three diagonal entries satisfy these conditions in degrees one, two, and three. At infinity in the diagonal variables the leading equations are the elementary symmetric polynomials e₁=e₂=e₃=0. They have no common projective point, so the affine fiber is finite. The resulting anchor variety has three free parameters, with finite algebraic choices of the diagonal entries.

Let B and C be the unique directions in L with off-diagonal top rows (1,0,0) and (0,1,0). Then B, C, and J−A are independent and span L. Conversely any solution of the following identity gives a three-space:

    per(u A + j J + x B + y C) = 24 (u+j)⁴.

Each of B,C has seven unknown physical entries after its upper off-diagonal row is fixed. First-order expansion in x,y gives four linear equations for each direction. The first at J eliminates the lower-block trace. The isolated spectral root gives the top diagonal b as a polynomial of the anchor. Write K(S) for the 3×3 matrix of unsigned 2×2 permanental cofactors, K′ for the coefficient of j in K(S+jJ₃), p for the off-diagonal top row, and D=R_S(−λ), a fixed nonzero constant. Then

    D b = 2λ pᵀK(S)1 − λ² pᵀK′1 + 2λ³ pᵀ1.

Indeed the block permanent is (a+j)P(j)+(p+j1)ᵀK(S+jJ₃)(p+j1). Its Borel transform evaluated at −λ removes every first-order contribution from the varying lower block. The remaining two first-order equations are linear in the five lower-block entries after trace elimination.

More generally set M=A+xB+yC, with top row (λ+b(x,y),p(x,y)) and lower block S+Y(x,y). In R_M(−λ), the contribution from the block diagonal part is b(x,y) R_{S+Y}(−λ). The cross terms contain at least one explicit p. Therefore the three quadratic coefficients in x,y are linear in the lower slopes Y for a fixed anchor, and the four cubic coefficients are quadratic in those slopes. Generically the six first-order equations for the two lower blocks and the three second-order isolated-root equations have rank nine in twelve lower slopes. On this rank-nine locus there are only three free lower slopes and three free anchor parameters. Lower ranks require separate treatment. No rank is assumed in the physical input generator.

The script fresh_laguerre_row.py uses two anchor diagonal parameters and three anchor off-diagonals, two top diagonals, and five lower parameters for each of B and C. Its 17 variables have no variable denominators. The first two equations fix the anchor invariants after trace elimination. Two further equations enforce the displayed top-diagonal formula. All remaining permanent coefficients come from the 24 physical permutations; the three quadratic and four cubic isolated-root combinations are included separately. Each other row imposes a four-by-four determinant with its own choice of Laguerre root. These determinant constraints remain necessary when that row has a singular projection. A calculation for a single root assignment does not cover the other assignments.

## Finite-field diagnostic and elimination

Let Tᵢ(S)=Dβᵢ(S) in the polynomial formula above. The five differential rows dR₀,dR₁,dR₂,dT₁,dT₂ act on six symmetric lower entries. When this matrix has rank five, its kernel is spanned by one lower-only perturbation κ. The degree-two root equation prescribes the symmetric part of the two-by-two matrix dT applied to the two unknown slopes. Choosing its skew parameter α, write the slopes as B₀(α)+δκ and C₀(α)+εκ. For each chosen linear combination of the top directions, the full lower perturbation thus belongs to a projective line. Its common-direction tests are quadratics on this line. The first two are the second-order polar at A and at J. The third comes from the cubic polar at A−λJ. Its homogeneous numerator vanishes on all directions with top row zero, so it has a factor of the top-row scale; after division, it too is quadratic. Fixed-degree binary quadratic resultants of any two give quartics in α. The existence of a target forces all these quartics to share α. Rank five of their coefficient matrix rules this out.

At prime 11, each of the two roots 3 and 10 gives 1,200 rational lower anchors. Every anchor had first-jet rank five and resultant rank five when all three quadratics were used. At prime 19 and λ=18 there were 7,008 anchors. Sixty-six had first-jet rank four and 48 rank-five anchors had all available fixed-degree resultant rows zero. The latter can occur from a common point at infinity in the fiber, and these anchors remain unresolved by the fixed-degree test. These censuses concern rational anchors only. At prime 461 and λ=21, the jet-rank-deficient locus has affine dimension one over the algebraic closure, by a seven-equation five-variable Gröbner calculation.

A pivot-free circuit for the same resultants uses five linear equations on seven homogeneous coordinates (top-row scale and six lower entries). The signed five-by-five complementary minors give its antisymmetric Plücker matrix P. For quadratic matrices Q and R, the common-direction resultant is

    tr(Q P R P)² − tr(Q P Q P) tr(R P R P).

This follows by restricting to the line and computing the discriminant of the binary determinant of the pencil of two forms. The circuit fresh_laguerre_plucker.py has 4,208 nodes at prime 40,961 and λ=885; all three outputs were checked against direct 24-permutation, two-quadratic resultants at nine choices of slope and skew parameter on the diagonal anchor modulo 461. The uneliminated 17-variable input at prime 461 reached a degree-six F4 matrix before hitting a 390 MB address-space limit. Neither computation gives a full-chart obstruction.
