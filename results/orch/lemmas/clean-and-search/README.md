L-meta-1904: small monotone-circuit synthesis checks

These are finite computational experiments, not Lean proofs. No general lower bound of the form edges plus vertex-cover size minus one is assumed. Star-cover circuits are constructed explicitly, then exhaustively checked as safe upper bounds.

The SAT encoding permits at most the requested number of gates. Each gate chooses AND, OR, or the constant true (empty AND). Repeated inputs are allowed. A source equal to the next wire index denotes the circuit convention of reading false from a nonexistent wire. This also represents empty OR, unary gates, and all out-of-range sources. Commutative inputs may be sorted. The final output is the last gate: shorter circuits can discard an unused suffix and append OR copies of their output.

Only minimal true assignments and maximal false assignments are encoded. AND/OR circuit outputs are monotone, so equality on those boundary assignments determines the whole graph function. For the safe variant, every AND output is constrained to be false on maximal false assignments, and constant-true gates are prohibited. Again, monotonicity propagates safety to every false assignment. OR-copy padding preserves safety.

Every returned witness is checked on all assignments, independently of the SAT encoding, including the safety of every AND output. An UNSAT result is only the solver's numerical verdict until separately certified in Lean.

Cases include C5, C6, C7, the seven-vertex theta graph with path lengths 2,3,3, and an eight-vertex shared-OR construction. Triangle absence and 4-cycle absence are exhaustively checked. Solver runs use one core, a 45-second time limit, and a 300 MB RSS cap.

Initial results: C5 at most six gates is UNSAT, C6 at most seven gates is UNSAT. Explicit safe witnesses use seven and eight gates respectively, and independently satisfy every clause of the generated upper-bound CNFs. C5 and C6 are known-case baselines; accepted site results already cover cycles. C7 at most nine, theta233 at most ten, and shared_or8 at most twelve gates all returned UNKNOWN at the 45-second limit for both ordinary and safe modes. UNKNOWN is not evidence of a lower bound.

The auxiliary unsafe mode asks for some AND gate to be true on a maximal false assignment. C5 at most seven gates with such an unsafe AND is UNSAT; C6 at most eight is UNKNOWN after 45 seconds. The unsafe encoding also permits the empty AND. No general normalization theorem is inferred from these finite checks.

The gen-used variant requires every nonfinal gate to be referenced later. This preserves existence with at most the size bound: remove dead gates and add output OR copies to pad to the requested number of gates. The gen-exposed variant additionally fixes the first gate to the OR of opposite C6 vertices 0 and 3; it tests only that structural route to a theta233 ten-gate circuit, not general circuit existence.

The two 120-second used-gate runs also returned UNKNOWN (theta233≤10 and shared_or8≤12). Their original active log filenames overlapped the first suite in scratch; all first-suite logs had already been archived and remain intact. The later logs are archived with used- prefixes, matching the revised reproduction script. The unrun gen-aut/run-aut experiment additionally reduces first-gate input choices by exhaustive input-graph automorphisms. This transformation is complete because relabeling all input wires by a graph automorphism preserves the target function.

Local theta233 repair: the ten-gate snapshot from the independent counterexample agent computes the target plus exactly the unwanted endpoint pair{0,1}. For each of the 45 unordered pairs of gate positions, run-local frees both operations and all their sources while fixing the other eight gates. All 45 instances are UNSAT (2.47 seconds total). This only rules out modifications of at most two gate descriptors in that exact ordered snapshot.

Exposed-OR route: an eight-gate C6 circuit containing OR of opposite inputs 0 and 3 would yield a ten-gate theta233 circuit by adjoining the new two-edge path. The selected OR can be moved first because it reads only inputs. gen-exposed fixes that first gate. Both ordinary and safe eight-gate cases are UNSAT (3.33 and 1.80 seconds respectively). This does not rule out other theta233 constructions.

The gen-lex variant combines the used-gate and automorphism symmetries with lexicographic order of adjacent independent gates, using descriptors(operation,left source,right source). If the second gate does not read the first output, the gates can be swapped; later references are renamed, and invalid sources are reset to the current nonexistent wire. The moved-earlier gate's invalid-source index can only decrease. Thus choosing the lexicographically least representation enforces these clauses. Future UNSAT conclusions from this variant depend on this mathematical completeness argument; it has not been formalized in Lean.

Positive symmetry validation: canonical-upper.py finds canonical topological orders of the explicit safe witnesses over every graph automorphism, verifies all truth assignments and every AND output, and validate-lex-upper-cnf.py then verifies that each resulting witness satisfies every clause in the actual symmetry-strengthened CNF. All five upper-bound instances pass. This is a useful implementation check, not a formal completeness proof for the symmetry rules.

Final outcome: theta233 at most ten gates and shared_or8 at most twelve gates also return UNKNOWN after 120 seconds each with the combined symmetries. No counterexample was found. All 61 SAT runs are complete: 50 numerical UNSAT results (including 45 restricted two-gate repairs) and 11 UNKNOWN results. No result in this folder is a Lean proof or a general lower bound. No Lean process was run for this task. The gen-aut/run-aut variant was prepared but not run as a separate suite; its clauses are included in the tested gen-lex suite. All other completed experiment logs are present in full. CNFs remain in scratch and can be regenerated deterministically from the archived source and data.
