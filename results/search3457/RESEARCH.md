## Source facts

Tarjan section 4 exhibits fourteen conjunction outputs on eleven variables, computed by sixteen ANDs and one OR, whereas AND-only computation requires eighteen gates. His Theorem 7, attributed to Lamagna and Savage, lower-bounds AND count by the sum of support sizes minus the number of outputs when distinct supports overlap in at most one variable. Its proof passes through his set-network transformation and charges distinct unions to distinct outputs. Section 5 applies it to projective-plane line conjunctions. The paper's network model has binary gates and simultaneous outputs, with input variables available for free. By Boolean duality the theorem also lower-bounds OR count for simultaneous sums, even when ANDs are allowed. Empty supports should be omitted when applying this formulation, and singleton outputs contribute zero. These are source-supported statements; the scan's OCR mangles several inequality signs, so use the proof rather than treating the OCR literally.

Jukna reports the Amano–Maruoka multioutput quadratic single-level separation ratio 29/28. His single-output counterexamples use saturated extensions: both sides of a bipartite graph become cliques. They therefore do not furnish C4-free bipartite examples. He distinguishes matching an edge graph on two-hot inputs from computing its full quadratic function. The stated edge-count lower bound for K2,2-free graphs concerns single-level circuits, not arbitrary monotone circuits. The modern status of the historical open lower-bound question is UNVERIFIED; no current resolution was found or claimed.

## Own derivation: why the natural Tarjan adaptations fail

Dualize Tarjan's conjunction construction to Boolean sums. With X = x1 OR x2 OR x3, the useful nonlinear intermediate is

    (X OR y) AND (X OR z) = X OR (y AND z).

It is true on each x_i singleton. Thus the raw construction fails the given singleton-clean-AND assumption.

Now replace each of its eleven source variables v by a fresh disjoint matching edge a_v AND b_v. This adds eleven gates to the seventeen-gate construction, producing fourteen quadratic outputs in twenty-eight gates. A single-level construction needs one edge-producing AND per matching edge and eighteen subsequent OR gates, giving twenty-nine. This is a mathematical reconstruction consistent with the reported ratio, not a claim that the inaccessible Amano–Maruoka article uses this exact realization.

Crucially, the reconstructed circuit is already clean on nonincident cross pairs. Every non-input wire is a monotone combination of genuine matching-edge products. On a nonincident cross pair all such products are false, so all subsequent gates are false. Any additional terms generated by the nonlinear AND have support at least four and contain real matching edges. L-meta-3457 permits such terms. Hence this separation does not refute its weaker requested normalization.

A second idea folds the fourteen raw linear outputs into one function by multiplying each by an output-selector variable and ORing the results. The resulting bipartite graph has two output neighborhoods sharing x1, x2 and x3, so it contains C4. Guarding variables to enforce singleton cleanliness does not remove that overlap. No C4-preserving selector construction with a cost separation was established.

## Own analysis: exact scope of the overlap theorem

Let the single quadratic edge function be

    F(x,y) = OR_{I(p,l)} (x_p AND y_l).

Putting y equal to the singleton vector e_l gives the linear output

    F(x,e_l) = OR_{I(p,l)} x_p.

C4-freeness implies that distinct neighborhoods N(l) intersect in at most one point. Tarjan's dual theorem therefore applies to a circuit that simultaneously computes all these neighborhood sums. After deleting isolated lines, its OR lower bound is the number of edges minus the number of nonisolated lines.

The missing transformation is quantitative. Restricting one quadratic circuit separately for every l gives at most N copies of that circuit. It does not produce one shared multioutput circuit with the original number of gates. A single vector gate representing all restrictions expands into several scalar gates unless one proves an additional sharing invariant. Likewise the original singleton condition is not inherited by individual cofactors: an AND x_p AND y_l becomes the input x_p after the l cofactor. More complicated ANDs may have different linear functions at different cofactors. Thus neither a direct scalar-gate charge nor a free shared-cofactor construction follows from singleton cleanliness.

A typed, syntactically bilinear or already normalized circuit may permit useful cofactor sharing. Assuming such a form for an arbitrary supplied circuit would assume the substance of the normalization being sought. No size-preserving cofactor transformation was proved. Applying Tarjan's theorem directly to the original single-output circuit is therefore unjustified.

The overlap theorem supplies a useful target invariant and explains why the raw Tarjan anomaly needs repeated variable overlap. It does not by itself prove the candidate stronger bound |E| + minimum_vertex_cover - 1 or L-meta-3457.
