# L-meta-971 mathematical strategy status

> Archive note: These are historical research-phase notes. Statements that no repository files were changed describe that phase, before this authorized archival checkpoint. Scratch paths and unpreserved search artifacts below are provenance, not archive dependencies.

The exact 27-gate lower bound for the 14-vertex, 21-edge Heawood edge function remains unproved and unrefuted. This agent has not changed the repository or claimed a target Lean proof.

The obvious upper bound is 27: choose one bipartition side as seven star centers, spend two OR gates for each three-neighbor list, one AND for each star, and six OR gates to combine the seven stars. Thus there are seven AND and twenty OR gates.

Its graph labeling is isomorphic to the current one. The proof is a large finite-model certificate, not a generic star-cover normalization.

* `Outside/L_meta_724.lean` defines an exact free-AND, counted-OR model (`OrCost.Meet` and `Run`), together with `paidMap`, `paidVertex`, and a degree-two `corePayments` deletion theorem. Its final graph has nine edges and needs eight OR gates. `prior_routes` owns closer study of this source.

## Missing normal form and why it cannot be assumed

A same-size reduction to a star-cover circuit would give `edges + minimumVertexCover - 1 = 27`, since the graph is bipartite and C4-free. Such a reduction has not been established. A first quadratic-producing AND can create a biclique containing nonedges, and later AND gates can filter those pair inputs. Higher-degree certificates can be harmless if they already contain a genuine graph edge. Therefore merely extracting first quadratic gates does not prove that all their rectangles lie in the graph.

The desired OR lower bound twenty is equivalent under De Morgan duality to an AND-count lower bound twenty for the monotone vertex-cover function, the conjunction of its twenty-one binary edge clauses. Internal CNF clauses of size greater than two can be ignored when tracking final binary clauses, but the remaining unit/edge system still allows dense intermediate rectangles to be filtered by later free OR operations. The unit sets used for filtering cost AND gates to construct; a complete accounting for that cost is missing.

## Deletion/payment idea

In the free-AND model, the first OR is the union of two monomials A and B. Restricting a variable in A minus B to zero makes that OR free. A critical edge probe on which the first OR agrees before and after the variable is added forces a second payment, exactly as in the existing degree-two theorem.

For the cubic graph, a third payment is needed. A useful restricted pattern, found by `prior_routes`, is when the first two relevant ORs are `v OR u` and `v OR r`, with all three vertices in the same bipartition side. Erasing v makes both ORs free. Because every other vertex on that side shares at most one neighbor with v, u and r cover at most two of v's three neighbors. Choose a neighbor w adjacent to neither. The independent probe `{u,r,w}` makes both ORs true, and adding v creates an edge while preserving both values. Applying `paidMap` to the remaining tail forces a third OR to disappear. Disjoint or nested second ORs remain untreated.

Restricted searches for a primary paper on the allowed GitHub host located no theorem supplying the needed Boolean normalization or OR bound. No external literature claim is used here.

## Checked counterexample to a proposed potential

`SafeMeetCounterexample.lean` compiles with an empty log and was independently audited by formal_audit. On the C4-free five-vertex path with edges ab,bc,cd,de, the two-OR prefix

    g = ab OR de
    h = g OR c

provides the three globally safe AND-meets g, b*h, and d*h. Their accepted edge families are respectively {ab,de}, {ab,bc}, and {cd,de}, which connect all four edges. Thus cumulative rank of the partition generated by safe available meets is three despite only two OR gates. One final OR combines b*h and d*h into the exact path function, using the expected three OR gates. This refutes the proposed partition potential, not the desired OR lower bound.

The earlier per-OR merging claim also fails: for the path with edges ab,bc,cd,af, take h0=cd OR bf, h1=h0 OR af, then g=a OR c. Before g all four target edges are separate under safe available meets; adding g simultaneously makes g*b merge ab/bc and g*h1 merge cd/af. The unsafe bf term carries credit from earlier ORs.

A proposed stronger single-output invariant subtracting the dimension of the span of four-cycle edge vectors remains unproved. Reusing a singleton difference a-b under contexts c,d gives differences ac-bc and ad-bd whose difference is a four-cycle vector, but intermediate nonedge and higher-degree monomials prevent simply restricting all such generators to target edges.
