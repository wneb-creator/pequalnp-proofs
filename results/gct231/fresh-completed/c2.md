# Transposition-stable three-planes are excluded

This is a scoped exact symbolic obstruction, not a Lean-checked proof and not a refutation of the unrestricted GCT-231 statement. It excludes every complex three-dimensional linear solution space stable under a transposition of vertices. Relabeling lets us use the transposition swapping 0 and 1.

All scripts and logs below are in `/tmp/oec/pequalnp-pilot-75/gct231/fresh/c2/`. The permanent is expanded exactly by summing its 24 terms. No numerical approximation enters the completed argument.

## Character decomposition and the four possible minus lines

Write a plus-character tangent matrix as

    P = [x, p, v, w;
         p, x, v, w;
         v, v, y, r;
         w, w, r, z],
    z = -2x-y-2p-4v-4w-2r.

The displayed expression for z is the degree-one permanent condition. A minus-character matrix is

    N = [a, 0, b, c;
         0,-a,-b,-c;
         b,-b, 0, 0;
         c,-c, 0, 0].

Its permanent identity is

    per(J+hN)-24 = -2h²(a²+4ab+4ac+4bc)+4h⁴b²c².

The quadratic has nonzero Gram determinant 12 before multiplication by -2. Thus a totally isotropic subspace of the three-dimensional minus space has dimension at most one. A nonzero solution minus line has bc = 0 and is one of

    (a,b,c) = (0,1,0), (-4,1,0), (0,0,1), (-4,0,1).

Swapping vertices 2 and 3 reduces these to the first two cases. Any stable solution three-plane therefore is either a plus three-plane or a plus two-plane together with one of these two minus lines.

## The minus line (0,1,0)

The coefficient of hk² in `per(J+hP+kN)` is `2(x-p)`. Therefore x = p throughout the plus two-plane. The coefficient of h²k² then vanishes identically. In particular, P has identical first two rows.

If p vanishes identically on the plus plane, its quartic permanent coefficient is `4v²w²`. The polynomial ring on the plane is a domain, so either v or w vanishes identically. In either case the remaining quadratic is nondegenerate on three variables: the Gram determinant of `-H₂/2` is 4. Such a space has no totally isotropic two-plane.

If p is nonzero on the plus plane, evaluate its affine line p = -1. The permanent reduces exactly to

    per(J+P) = 4(1+v)²(1+w)² = 24.

Both affine linear polynomials 1+v and 1+w must be nonzero constants on this line. Hence v = Ap and w = Bp on the whole plane. On its one-dimensional kernel of p, the quadratic condition is `y²+2ry-r² = 0`. A nonzero vector in that kernel has r nonzero, so (p,r) are coordinates on the plane. Write r = t and y = Cp+λt, where `λ²+2λ-1 = 0`.

Substituting these forms into the full permanent yields six coefficient equations in A, B, C, λ, after reduction modulo `λ²+2λ-1`. Together with that relation their exact Groebner basis is `[1]`. The equations and the result are printed by `c2_reductions.py` and retained in `c2_reductions.log`. This excludes the first minus-line case.

## The minus line (-4,1,0)

The coefficient of hk² forces

    x = p+8r-8v-8w+8y.

After this substitution the coefficient of h²k² is

    -16(r-w)(4p+19r-12v-12w+17y).

One linear factor must vanish identically on the plus two-plane. The two resulting four-dimensional plus spaces have nondegenerate quadratic forms `-H₂/2`, with Gram determinants 32832 and 5332 respectively in the script's coordinates. Every isotropic two-plane is represented in one of four hyperbolic-coordinate charts. On each chart, substituting into the cubic and quartic permanent coefficients gives an exact unit ideal. All four charts for each branch return `[1]`, as recorded in `mixed4a.log` and `mixed4b.log`.

## The entirely plus three-plane

On the six-dimensional plus tangent space, the quadratic form `-H₂/2` has Gram determinant 288 in coordinate order (p,v,w,r,y,x). An exact hyperbolic basis identifies it with `2Σ XᵢYᵢ`. For each of the eight choices swapping the two coordinates within any subset of the three hyperbolic pairs, a transverse maximal isotropic plane has `Y = SX`, with S a skew-symmetric three-by-three matrix.

The script substitutes this three-parameter expression into the cubic and quartic permanent coefficients. Their ten cubic and fifteen quartic coordinate coefficients have Groebner basis `[1]` on every chart. All eight completed outputs are in `plus.log`. Each chart computation took approximately 10–20 seconds.

## Why the hyperbolic charts cover

Let L have dimension n and be totally isotropic in a nondegenerate 2n-dimensional hyperbolic space. For any j of the n coordinate-pair groups, projection of L to those groups has rank at least j: its kernel is isotropic in the complementary nondegenerate space of dimension 2(n-j), so has dimension at most n-j.

This rank condition lets us select one coordinate functional from each pair, with the selected n functionals independent on L. The elementary independent-transversal argument is by induction: if a proper set of groups is rank-tight, solve it and then solve the complementary groups in the quotient; if none is tight, select any nonzero functional in the first group and quotient by it, leaving the rank condition for the remaining groups. Thus L is a graph in one of the 2ⁿ indicated charts. Isotropy of that graph is exactly the skew-symmetry of S.

This applies with n = 2 to the mixed (-4,1,0) branches and n = 3 to the entirely plus case. Consequently these unit ideals cover the full cases, not just generic planes.

The chart script uses exact algebraic extensions and checks both nondegeneracy of the coordinate transformation and vanishing of the quadratic after every chart substitution. An earlier direct `mixed0` odd-dimensional chart search timed out on its first chart and was stopped; it is not used in the argument. The elementary normalization above replaces it.

The order-two action is diagonalizable over the complex numbers. Combining its character decomposition, the complete minus-line list, and the three exclusions above rules out all transposition-stable solution three-planes. Arbitrary planes without this symmetry remain outside this result.
