# Literature check for L-gct-231

Read date: **2026-11-25 (UTC)**. Research was read-only through the web tool; no shell egress, submissions, or credential access. This is a bounded literature check, not an exhaustive bibliography.

**Outcome:** I did not find a published theorem that resolves the existence of the required complex linear subspace through the all-ones matrix in the symmetric permanent fibre. The closest accessible results concern homogeneous zero fibres, substantially different dimension ranges, or real positivity. None justifies a new restriction on the candidate subspace in the current proof.

## Primary sources actually read

### Chan and Ilten: Fano schemes of determinants and permanents

- Full paper: <https://arxiv.org/pdf/1312.2577>
- Abstract/version record: <https://arxiv.org/abs/1312.2577>
- Read: introduction, the compression-component statements around Corollary 5.6, and portions of the elementary matrix arguments in Section 3.

This paper works over an algebraically closed field, with a characteristic restriction for permanents. It studies linear spaces in the common **zero locus of minors or permanents of a generic matrix**, whose entries are independent variables. Corollary 5.6 gives a compression-component classification under the explicit inequality `k > (r - 2)n + 1`; our small direction space is outside that hypothesis. Its detailed small-matrix classification concerns the generic case, not the marked symmetric nonzero fibre required here.

Useful method: expand permanents in linear-form entries and exploit special support patterns. Limitation: neither the compression classification nor the torus-fixed-point argument permits assuming that an arbitrary candidate through the prescribed all-ones matrix has a zero row or another compression pattern.

### Ilten and Suss: Fano schemes for generic sums of products of linear forms

- Full paper: <https://arxiv.org/pdf/1610.06770>
- Abstract/version record: <https://arxiv.org/abs/1610.06770>
- Read: abstract/introduction and Example 4.5 concerning the generic four-by-four permanent.

Example 4.5 uses the high-dimensional zero-row and zero-column linear spaces of the generic permanental hypersurface to obtain a product-rank obstruction. Those are spaces in the homogeneous **zero** fibre. The result does not classify small affine spaces in the symmetric fibre through the all-ones matrix. The generic sums-of-products hypotheses likewise cannot be transferred to the symmetric specialization without proof.

This is relevant background for a Fano-scheme approach, but supplies no valid coordinate-normalization shortcut for this task.

### Yabe: Bi-polynomial rank and determinantal complexity

- Full paper: <https://arxiv.org/pdf/1504.00151>
- Read: Section 3.1 and Section 3.2, particularly Lemma 3.1 and the proof of the real-field result.

Section 3.1 discusses the algebraic Hessian-rank method. Section 3.2 explicitly works **over the real numbers** and strengthens an obstruction using the signature of a real symmetric Hessian. That signature argument does not apply to arbitrary complex symmetric matrices. Its base point and determinantal-complexity question also differ from the present marked fibre.

Practical implication: algebraic polar forms can give necessary equations over the complex numbers; positivity or a sum-of-real-squares conclusion cannot be imported. A complex symmetric matrix is not a Hermitian matrix.

### Chau: Permanental ideals of symmetric matrices

- Full paper: <https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.03367>
- Abstract/version record: <https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.03367>
- Read: introduction, Lemma 2.3, and the minimal-prime description in Section 4, especially Theorem 4.1.

The paper studies the ideal generated by **every two-by-two permanent** of a symmetric matrix, in characteristic different from two. Its elementary ideal-membership identities and minimal-prime description force strong support restrictions once all those permanents vanish. The listed components are supported in a principal two-by-two block with its permanent relation.

This is the most directly relevant source for a possible short support-factorization argument. However, the present constant-permanent hypothesis initially gives coefficient sums and polarized identities, not individual vanishing of every two-by-two permanent. Establishing that missing implication, or a suitable analogous implication, would be essential. The paper does not provide it for the current fibre.

### Pant, Chakrabartty, and Singh: A permanental analog of rank-nullity

- Full paper: <https://arxiv.org/pdf/2507.01344>
- Published record: <https://drops.dagstuhl.de/entities/document/10.4230/LIPIcs.STACS.2026.70>
- Read: abstract and the scope statements in the introduction.

The advertised rank-nullity classes include symmetric nonnegative matrices, positive semidefinite matrices, and adjacency matrices of balanced signed graphs. These conditions do not cover arbitrary complex symmetric matrices. Consequently this theorem does not supply the needed dimension bound or a justified reduction to ordinary nonsingular-matrix space theorems.

## Exact geometric reformulation: our derivation, not a sourced theorem

Write the prescribed all-ones matrix as `J`. If a linear space `L` satisfies `per(J + t) = 24` for every `t` in `L`, homogeneity gives

`per(s J + t) = 24 s^4`

for every nonzero complex `s`, because `t / s` belongs to `L`. The same identity at zero follows by equality of polynomials in `s`. Thus the appropriate homogeneous ambient hypersurface is

`per(A) - 24 s^4 = 0`,

and the relevant linear subspace consists of `(s J + t, s)`. It contains the prescribed marked point `[J : 1]`. The ordinary Fano scheme of `per(A) = 0` records only its intersection with `s = 0`; it loses the lower-degree coefficient conditions that are central here. General row and column scalings do not preserve the marked point or the fixed nonzero fibre, so their torus action cannot establish a compression assumption for this problem.

This reformulation explains the gap between the retrieved Fano results and the current task. It is not a proof of existence or nonexistence.
